# Straws

Some of the most captivating stories my grandmother used to tell me as a child were of this beautiful, bucolic paradise. I've heard myths of towering trees with verdant leaves, of crystalline water that tasted divine, and of gorgeous birds that sang enchantments as they soared through the fluffy clouds. I still remember how she would weave her tales with such exquisite detail as if she were a projector playing movies on a screen, and sigh with the smiles of a dozen souls as she paused to clear her throat. "Where is this place? It sounds heavenly," I would ask her. She would chuckle lightly, and her face would take on a smile—a somber one, as though it were an unpleasant one to have. "These are merely legends, places that we want to visit but in reality could never have," she would say as she sat in her wooden chair overlooking the field of flowers to which she had dedicated her whole life.

Those stories, like the times and places they described, are now long gone. My grandmother moved away to a distant land that my family told me I could not access; I knew they were bluffing and that she actually had died. I usually can't get a good night's rest while dreaming of my grandmother, so I look outside of my bedroom window: not a single soul roaming in the streets, which was to be expected considering dawn was still a couple of hours away. *Great, I guess I'm awake now*, I groan to myself as I roll out of bed and lazily throw on some clothes. Today was going to be a typical day with typical activities: do some chores, complete some tasks, buy some groceries. I sigh as I walk toward the front door and grab my jacket. It was a tad boring to be doing this every day—waking at the start of the day, living during the day, and retiring at night—but at least my life was stable, predictable, and calm.

I open the door and look outside. High above my house—high above all of the other houses in all of the other neighborhoods—stood a collection of lampposts, each one adorned with images of people having fun or consuming various items. The Mind ordered these kinds of banners to be placed on every tall ledge to encourage people to invest in what it calls its "provisions." To be honest, I find them a little unnerving, as if they are transmitting messages to my mind and slowly invading my subconscious. I shift my gaze away from the posters to the ground below—more specifically, to the small piles of garbage along the streets. In the early days of the regime, people used to decry the growing amount of litter around the country; in response, the Mind told us not to worry and that it helps the environment immeasurably. I didn't believe the Mind, but every time I raised my concerns to other people they would stare at me malevolently as if I said something incredibly offensive. I'd heard that the Mind did terrible things to whistleblowers, so I stopped. I take one more second, just to take in the dimly-lit scenery and the faint whirring of a nearby factory, before stepping out into the darkness. At least I thought that's what I had walked into.

I could've blinked; I could've died. That time was all a blur. But the next thing I remember is that I was now in a bright world—a bright, foul-smelling world. In me, nothing was comprehending anything that was comprehensible: left was right, red was blue, to blossom was to wither, to decay was to bloom. In the midst of the cacophonous maelstrom of entropy and confusion, there was only one very faint thing that could be grasped. A sensation of hope, a harbinger of destruction. A voice.

*Thank you for joining Operation Straws. It is my pleasure to welcome you to the new world order.*

----------

*Straws* is a two-dimensional (2D) platformer that tells the unfortunate story of a world overrun by the evils of garbage and the fortunate story of a shrewd, compassionate player that works to solve that mystery—you. Set in a near-future, distant universe that is very similar to ours but has been bewitched by a malicious force, you must traverse the terrain, survive the evils, and uncover the secrets that consumed the world you were transported to—the same secrets that could consume your own.
